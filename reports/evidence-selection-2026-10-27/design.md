# One bounded candidate

The known failures contain both document and passage ranking errors. Document-body prefix coverage repeats source-identification words in covers and SMS lists, while the substantive fee passage lacks “RTI” and “online”. The existing reciprocal-rank fusion further favors passages present in both channels. A within-document novelty trial was rejected previously; it is not reused here.

This candidate retains the exact keyword/dense candidate pool and reciprocal-rank score. It replaces only the 0.05 coverage component with a normalized field-weighted lexical score. Document titles provide a small source-context contribution, and body term frequency is saturated and length-normalized. The score operates on complete words rather than five-letter prefixes, so “notify” cannot match “notifications” merely by sharing a prefix. It scores every candidate independently and can change both document and passage ordering. There are no topic, question, source, page or passage-specific rules.

Fixed parameters before the one evaluation: body frequency weight 1, title presence weight 0.5, frequency saturation 1.2, body length normalization 0.75, candidate-pool inverse frequency log(1+(N-df+0.5)/(df+0.5)), normalized lexical contribution 0.05. These are one predetermined specification, not a grid search. Existing authority preference, candidate limits, duplicate filter, two-passage-per-document cap and prompt limits remain intact.

All original nine and the previously inspected fifteen held-out questions are now known development/regression evidence. The twenty new source-reviewed questions were frozen in commit 4682f10 before candidate implementation or assessment. `freeze.json` pins the exact fixture. Expected evidence, including all prior expected sets and relative-order constraints, will not change after results are seen. The full-text source review used a disposable copy; the source fingerprint remained unchanged after child exit.
