

2020 was the year no one saw coming. The Pandemic that started in March 2020 will
forever be a stand-out period in our history books. It was a time when the economy
seemed to be brought to its knees, when the whole world experienced immense suffering,
while simultaneously banning together to find strength, resilience, and community to
help each other make it through this difficult period in our history. 
When the pandemic first started ActforGoa, saw that there was an immediate need to
help the people of Goa access information that they could act upon. A lockdown that the
people of India believed was only supposed to be for a couple of days overnight became
the beginning of a two-month quarantine. People were left unprepared for this suddenly
extended lockdown. For the first week to ten days, people didn't have stable access to
food supplies, as the Goa government shut down all the grocery stores imposing a home
food delivery system that didn't yet exist in the state. In the beginning, ActforGoa used our
network and resources to help people find ways to get food and alleviate their anxiety
about the situation by giving updates in real-time about the nature of the lockdown, Covid
19, and all the policies that were being developed in response to the same. 
As quarantine rules in the state evolved and started to relax, the general public was finally
able to access food easily. At this time the larger need of the hour switched to supporting
the immense migrant population in Goa, this population was believed to have more than
200,000 people living in Goa at the time. These individuals survive off their daily wages
and when these people were suddenly left unemployed with little to no savings to help
them make it through the lockdown, they were left them facing potential starvation.
Volunteers across the state banded together to support one another and this community.
It was inspiring to see the number of Civil Society Organizations, Nonprofits, Businesses,
and individuals who came together to insure the elderly or disabled were looked after,
and that the migrants were fed. ActforGoa began coordinating between all the various
groups working across the state to try and help streamline the feeding programs, liaison
with the government, and insure every group was being fed. For almost two and half
months these volunteer groups helped feed and eventually send home over 90,000
migrants across the state, 
The first lockdown finally came to an end in mid-May when the State and Goa were
announced Covid free, Goa quickly became a safe haven for people from across the
country. While the rest of the country continued to wrestle with lockdowns throughout
2020 into 2021, Goa stayed mostly open as the number of Covid cases here seemed to stay
manageable compared to the rest of the country. ActforGoa continued to monitor the
situation and gave regular updates on the same. At this time we decided to return some of
our focus back to our original intention of helping the community take informed action
for the sustainable development of the state. We launched a fellowship program in the fall
of 2020 and supported our first fellow Cecile Rodriguez with her Rosto Goa campaign, We
also brought on Trella Rodrigues to the team to continue to grow and work with the
network of changemakers we had built during the pandemic to educate and empower
them to further act for the good of the community.

To create a mutually beneficial supportive arrangement between
ActforGoa and active citizens doing great work towards the
sustainability of Goa, Act for Goa was able to support Cecile Rodriguez
an active citizen from Goa who has been moving around the state and
identifying speed breakers that can be threatening to riders and
drivers through a campaign called #RostoGoa. Through this
campaign, we helped Cecille mobilize hundreds of people to
download the PinIt app and map potholes, unpainted speed breakers,
broken or missing lights, and safety mirrors, broken or missing
signage, the data of which was gathered to be provided to local
authorities to support improvements in Road safety.
During the course of the fellowship, Act for Goa connected her to
SaveLIFE Foundation, an independent NGO focused on improving road
safety and emergency medical care across India to discuss the
ratification of a road safety policy for Goa.
The fellowship concluded with Cecille handing over gathered data
from the village of Taleigao to the junior engineer of Taleigao
Panchayat.
