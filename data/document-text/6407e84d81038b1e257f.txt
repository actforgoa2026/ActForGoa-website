

2021 -2022 saw us head into our second full year of the pandemic. The second wave of Covid
hit hardest across India, as most of the medical systems across the country including Goa
had not properly built up their capacity or infrastructure to deal with the sudden rise in
cases. The second wave hit at the tail end of the tourist season. This time around the main
crisis people were facing in Goa was access to medical treatment. While the state did go into
lockdowns at various points as the numbers climbed they were never as strict as the first
lockdown, which meant that construction sites were allowed to continue work, and
migrants weren't left suddenly without an income or way to get home. This meant the need
of the hour was leveraging ActforGoa as an information platform to help people understand
safety protocols, treatment protocols, where to access beds, oxygen, and even food delivery
for people sick. Even though Goa has the highest hospital bed per capita ratio in the country,
there were a few days when people struggled to find beds and there were oxygen shortages.
The community was vigilantly watching everything unfold though and within days an
investigation was called for and an appeal through the high courts helped rectify the
situation. Luckily by the time the monsoon set in fully the number of cases had started to
decline and the medical infrastructure in Goa improved. 
As the second wave began to recede ActForGoa refocused our attention on the various
campaigns we had planned to run on a monthly basis. We also kept an ear to the ground
and tried to make timely responses to issues that would affect the sustainable development
of Goa. In 2021, Goa saw two disastrous weather events. The first was on May 16th when
Cyclone Tauktae hit Goa. The amount of damage was enormous and parts of the state were
without power for a week. Later the state electricity department revealed the storm cost it
Rs. 25 crores. The heavy rainfall on July 22 which caused severe floods was the second one
when 400 people were forced to evacuate and two trains were derailed from the tracks. We
helped spread information about flood relief during and after the heavy rainfall. Weather
patterns over the years in the state have shown us that the trend for more storms and heavy
rainfall will increase. This means floods will sadly continue to be a regular occurrence every
monsoon, and farmers will have more difficulty keeping their crops alive in years to come. It
is vital that as a state and community, we respond to this information with better-designed
infrastructure and disaster response plans.  We hope that over time collaboratively we will
be able to work with the community and the government to create a climate-resilient and
adaptive development strategy and emergency response plan. 
By the end of the monsoon, the state luckily seemed to get a reprieve from natural disasters
and covid spikes. ActforGoa then refocused our energy on building our network of
changemakers and creating content to inspire action. This year we decided to skip the Insta
lives and instead focus on making short and long-format informative videos on specific
topics we felt were of interest and importance to the community. Moving forward we will
continue to create films like these, in addition to the infographics, and blog articles we write.
We want to find ways to share important information with every member of the community.
Sadly 2022 started with the third wave of COVID right when state-level elections were
kicking off. Luckily the new variant OMICRON wasn't nearly as deadly but was far more
contagious, which meant it seemed as if most of the state was out for the first month or two
of the year. ActforGoa was grateful that in some way we could help support the community
in navigating this challenging year.
