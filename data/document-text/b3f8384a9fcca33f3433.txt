 
SOP for the Home Isolation on Government of Goa- IMA Goa State 
PPP Model for COVID-19 patients 
1. On recommendation of the Deputy Collector a COVID-19 positive patient will 
be assessed by the Health Officer/Medical Officer for the suitability of his 
health status for home isolation. 
 
2. The COVID-19 positive patient will be asked to report to the concerned 
health facility at a specified time where he/she will be screened for any 
existing co-morbid condition, his/her regular medical treatment if any etc. 
 
3. Patients recommended for Home isolation will be provided an “IMA Covid -
19 Kit” (Medical Supplies and Biomedical waste handling)  made available by 
IMA Goa state at a cost price, the same shall be purchased from the local 
body Municipality/Panchayat at a price of Rs 2500/-, the details of the kit and 
IEC material is enclosed in Annexure G for reference. 
 
4. The local body Municipality/Panchayat will be informed to collect biomedical 
waste generated from the home isolation facility at the end of the seventeen 
day for its appropriate disposal as per the Goa State Pollution Control 
Guidelines through an authorized agency hired by them. 
5. Instruction for patients in home isolation as annexed (Annexure A) 
 
6. Concerned Health Officer/Medical Officer shall designate IMA doctor within 
the jurisdiction as per the list made available by IMA Goa State. The onboard 
IMA Doctor shall be accessible via telephone to the patient and care giver at 
all times and shall pass on th e relevant  medical information in-case of 
medical emergency to Concerned Health Officer/Medical Officer for further 
course of action including shifting of the patient to nea rest Covid -19 care 
facility/Covid-19 Hospital. The role of designated IMA doctor shall be limited 
to facilitating telephonic consultation as and when required and updating 
the Concerned Health Officer/Medical Officer  on t he health status  of the 
patient. Designated IMA doctor shall not be liable for any medico-legal action 
in-case of any eventuality arising from the above circumstances. 
 
7. Concerned Health Officer/Medical Officer shall intimate the designated IMA 
doctor about the  recommendation of home isolation and shall furnish 
information of the patient including, Name, address, telephone number, and 
any relevant medical history if available, the details of the designated IMA 
doctor including name and telephone number shall be also made available 
to the patient. 
 
8. Instruction for Care giver and attendants as annexed (Annexure B) 
9. Instruction for Self-monitoring as annexed (Annexure C) 
10. A person should stay in Self-Isolation for seventeen (17) days from the date 
of COVID-19 positive test. He/She should not have had any symptoms for at 
least 10 days to end the self-isolation. 
11.  Format for the Health Status Certificate of COVID-19 for home isolation. 
(Annexure D) 
12.  Prescribed format for the register to be maintained at PHC/CHC/UHC 
(Annexure E) 
13. Home Monitoring Isolation Chart (Annexure F) 
14.  Incase of Emergencies, the dedicated vehicle to be made available by the 
district administration at each hospital CHC/PHC/UHC for shifting of the 
home isolated patients to the nearest 24*7 health facility and further if 
required to the nearest COVID care centre/dedicate COVID Hospital. 
 
 
Annexure ‘A’  
1. Keep yourself hydrated. Drink at least 2 litres of water per day. 
2. Maintain isolation. Always wear a mask, should you step out of your room 
in unavoidable circumstances.  
3. Always use handkerchief/tissue and cover your mouth while coughing and 
sneezing. The used handkerchief should be stored in airtight polythene bag 
till washing. Used tissue should be disposed along with used masks in a 
yellow bag, which will be collected by officials from the local health centre 
at the end of your 17 days home isolation period. 
4.  Wash your hands frequently with soap and water for 40 to 60 seconds. 
5.  After using the toilet, close the commode before pressing the flush. Do not 
use cloth to wipe your hands dry. It is advisable to raise your hands and 
allow it to air dry. 
6.  After using the toilet, clean all the surfaces that you have touched and 
wash your hands with soap and water. 
7.  The patient should clean the isolation room himself/herself. If that is not 
possible the person who is cleaning should take all necessary precautions 
like wearing triple layer medical mask, gloves, face shield/goggles. The 
room should be cleaned with disinfectants (as per manufacturer guidelines) 
or bleaching powder mixed with water (3 spoons per litre) twice a day.  
8.  Avoid smoking as it may affect your recovery.  
9. Do not share personal items like utensils, towels, etc. with other family 
members.  
10. Soak used bedsheets, clothes, towels/utensils in hot water for 30 minutes 
before proceeding with normal cleaning/washing. The caretaker should 
always wear a triple layer medical mask and gloves while handling used 
items. Cloth masks/handkerchiefs used shall be soaked in 1% sodium 
hypochlorite solution for 20-30 minutes before washing. Wash them 
separately. Sundry the clothes/utensils after washing.  
11.  Monitor your health for raise in temperature or other symptoms. If the 
severity of the symptoms increase, report to your local health centre. 
12. The patients/care givers shall coordinate with the designated IMA doctor 
assigned, for any counseling related to co-morbidity or specific to COVID-
19. The patients are advised to contact the assigned IMA doctor only if 
required, the patient shall not withheld any medical information and shall 
cooperate at all times. 
 
Annexure ‘B’  
1. Wear a triple layer/reusable medical mask appropriately when in the same 
room with the patient. The front of the mask should not be touched or 
handled during use. If the mask gets wet or dirty with secretions, it must be 
changed immediately. After use, discard the mask by using the appropriate 
technique that is, do not touch the front, but instead untie it from behind 
and wash your hands thoroughly after disposal of the mask. Disinfect the 
mask in 1% hypochlorite solution before disposing off in a yellow 
biomedical waste bag provide in the kit.  
2.  Avoid touching your face, nose or mouth.  
3.  Ensure hand hygiene after coming in contact with the patient or the 
patients’ immediate environment. Wash your hands with soap and water 
for 40-60 seconds as often as possible.  
4. Alcohol-based hand rubs or sanitizers can be used, if hands are not visibly 
soiled. Always air dry the hands by raising up. Do not wipe with cloth.  
5. Wash your hands before and after preparing food, before eating, after 
using the toilet, and whenever your hands look dirty.  
6. Do not touch any objects in the patient’s room.  
7. Towels, bedsheets, utensils and dishes used by the patient should be kept 
separately and cleaned with soap/detergent and water, while wearing 
gloves. The utensils and dishes may be reused. Always wash hands after 
taking off gloves or handling used items.  
8. Always keep any items used by patients in hot water for 30 minutes before 
regular cleaning.  
9. Food and other items needed by the patient must be provided to them in 
their room only.  
10. Use triple later medical mask and disposable gloves while cleaning and 
handling surfaces, clothing or linen used by the patient.  
11. Ensure that the patient is taking all medication as per the doctor’s advice. 
12.  Incase there is any worsening of signs or symptoms, contact the local 
health centre. 
Annexure ‘C’  
1. Do health checks every morning, afternoon and night, as well as anytime 
you feel like you might have a fever, or are feeling unwell.  
2. 2. Check your temperature with a thermometer. In case of dependent 
patients, caregivers can monitor the temperature. Use a mask and gloves 
and wash your hands thoroughly before and after checking the 
temperature.  
3. Check your pulse and oxygen saturation using a pulse oximeter three times 
a day.  
4. Temperature, pulse, oxygen saturation should be recorded in the 
monitoring chart  
5. Incase of the following scenarios, please contact the local health center or 
assigned IMA doctor immediately: 
  Temp >100 degrees Fahrenheit 
  Pulse rate >100  
 Drop in oxygen saturation (SpO2 < 95%) 
  Difficulty in breathing 
  Weakness  
 Developing bluish discoloration of lips/face 
  Persistent pain/pressure in the chest  
 Mental confusion or inability to arouse
