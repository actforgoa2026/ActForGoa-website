Viksit Goa – Towards a Clean, Green and Prosperous Goa  
A Green Sustainable Viksit Goa 
Introduction 
These recommendations for a Green Viksit Goa1 have been put together with the involvement of 
over 20 expert contributors, using various existing sets of proposals including the Green 
Manifesto for Goa and the State Action Plan on Climate Change. The overarching principles 
have been endorsed by around 2000 people. 
The principles are: 
1/ Forward with Nature - For our well-being, our health, and our water & food security, 
PROTECT AND CARE for sacred mother earth:  forests, rivers & streams, plateaus, 
khazans,  wildlife, air and water quality, and our marine environment. Nature first equals 
People First. Climate resilience is of existential importance to people. 
2/ Community Ownership — DESIGN AND PLAN with local communities to build resilience 
and empower people to make strategic and appropriate decisions for our state and our 
villages, building skills and SELF-RELIANCE. 
3/ Local Green Economy — INCENTIVISE AND PROMOTE local green businesses and jobs 
including in sustainable fishing, farming and sustainable tourism, and low footprint, low 
infra-need  business and employment in areas like IT, consulting, green building 
design and renewable energy R & D making Goa a Centre of Excellence for a 
sustainable economy and employment opportunities. 
4/ Accountable Governance — ENFORCE to allow only legal and safe development and 
support TRANSPARENT and robust governance including  through tech-enabled 
enforcement,  data publication, and integrated cross-government working. 
5/ Cultural Identity — CELEBRATE AND PROTECT Goa’s culture, diversity of traditions, 
cuisine, music, and sustainable “sussegad” lifestyle as well as local knowledge on 
agriculture, water management and much more. 
The policies recommended cover all the 21 areas on which the government has sought 
feedback, and are extensive and broad. For this reason, we also highlight 5 URGENT & 
IMMEDIATE policy changes which are fundamental to a Green Viksit:

development goals, by 2047 which offers high qualit y of life, climate resilience in the face of

The Issues 
From these sources and other discussions, it is cle ar that Goa is facing a number of big issues 
which are compounding and are often not fully acknowledged: 
1. Increasing exposure to climate risk including heat stress, water scarcity, fires, flooding and 
coastal erosion exacerbated by extraction of groundwater and limited water management. 
● Temperature Changes: Goa's mean annual temperature has increased by over 1°C 
since the beginning of the 20th century (1901-2018) , which is higher than the 
national average.  
o Each 1¡C warming above optimal temperature correlates with 3-7% GDP reduction 
through multiple pathways: (1) reduced agricultural  productivity, (2) decreased 
labor efficiency, (3) increased energy costs for co oling, (4) infrastructure stress, 
and (5) health impacts reducing workforce productivity. 
4 
 
o Nature-based cooling solutions (urban forests, wetl and restoration) can reduce 
local temperatures by 2-8¡C through evapotranspirat ion and albedo effects, 
potentially saving ₹14,400-44,800 crores in avoided economic losses2. 
● Rainfall Patterns : Goa’s mean annual rainfall has increased by 68% from 1901-
2018. Inter-annual rainfall variability has also increa sed, and exceptionally heavy 
rainfall events (IMD category III) have increased by over 100%.  
● Sea Level Rise: Goa is already experiencing sea level rise. Long-term data indicates 
a sea level rise rate of 1.45 mm/year  (1969-2013), suggesting an increase of about 
0.48 ft (half a foot) over 100 years.  
o The cited 1.45 mm/year rate (1969-2013) represents historical linear trends, but 
recent satellite altimetry data shows acceleration.  Nerem et al. (2018) 
demonstrated that global sea level rise is accelerating at 0.084 – 0.025 mm/year², 
suggesting exponential rather than linear progression. 
● Extreme Weather Events : The state is vulnerable to more frequent and seve re 
extreme events such as droughts, cyclones, floods, and untimely rainfall.  
 
2. Destruction of our natural environment  including pollution of air, water and soil through  
increased volumes of traffic, poor waste management and inappropriate sanitation facilities. 
Deforestation and loss of green cover harms ecosyst ems and lead to loss of biodiversity, 
which is resulting from unplanned urbanisation and land-intensive  institutions and 
infrastructure. Climate Change increases the vulnerability to forest fires, seawater intrusion 
and coral bleaching. Increased urbanisation is creating a heat island effect.  
3. Harm to our architectural heritage, social fabric a nd identity  through unplanned and 
extensive construction and poor design; 
4. Changing demographics with second home developments, migrant workers, and an ageing 
local population as well as an influx of new residents  retiring, starting businesses or working 
remotely 
5. Work opportunities pose challenges. Traditional emp loyment opportunities in fishing 
and agriculture are less appealing  to the younger generation than they were in the pa st, 
newer employment opportunities are often out of rea ch to local people (some of whom 
may feel that businesses are not fully inclusive), and AI and new technologies are putting jobs 
across the world at risk, leading to outward migration anknld insecurity about the future. 
6. Newer sectors like tourism, mining and some industr y have negative environmental 
consequences in terms of water usage, deforestation and pollution. Tourism is also affected 
by coastal erosion and pollution and the attractive coastal ecology is at risk from salination.  
Together these pressures on Goa’s ecology, society, culture, and economy, with governance not 
adapting to the challenge, are compounding to create rapid, irreversible change and destruction 
to GoaÕs landscapes, identity, quality of life and economic security. 
The Opportunity 
This multi-pronged crisis offers numerous opportunities in building a local green economy - good 
for quality of life and economic security as well a s landscapes and identity. This would be in line

1. Agriculture, Fisheries & Livestock 
Issues 
● Fisheries are threatened by over-fishing and depletion of f ish stocks in Goa’s 
coastal waters – both by external trawlers and Goa’s own fishing fleet.  Fishing 
practices have deteriorated with little effort to return by-cat ch and the closed 
period not being full reflected, as well as LED-fishing which negatively impacts 
fish behaviour and disrupt migratory and other patterns. 
● Goa’s beaches are a major tourist attraction and the d evelopment of the 
hospitality facilities has had negative side effects  on the marine ecosystems. 
Galgibaga Beach and Grande and Bat Islands are nationa lly significant 
conservation locations 
● Practices such as burning stubble, other agro-wastes and brush are degrading 
Goa’s soils and hillsides as forest fires increase. Soil erosion al so results from 
heavy rainfall on exposed unprotected land. 
● The use of pesticides, chemical fertilisers, herbicide/weedicide (a gro 
chemicals) and monoculture plantations to earn more profit and reduce labour 
costs are damaging to the health of soil and surrounding ecosystems including 
wetlands and to health. Eutrophication as a result of run-off of fertilisers is also 
destroying the ecological health of our water bodies. The i ncreased used of 
weedicide and its residues in runoff is slowly contaminating the wetlands.  
● Agricultural fields including Khazans and associated salt pans are falling into 
disuse or harmed by salinisation resulting from aquaculture or over -extraction 
of groundwater. Poor maintenance of bunds and sluice is helping the mangrove 
species to colonize the earlier cultivated areas thus reducing the area available 
for cultivation.  
● Goa’s rich cultural history and sophisticated community level organ isation has 
revolved around traditional forms of agriculture including khazan  lands, agro-
fisheries and the cultivation of traditional varieties of gra ins. Social capital and 
skills relating to agriculture like  maintenance of bunds and  bandaras is being 
lost, as the economy becomes dependent on tourism/second home 
construction. This is affecting Goa’s longer term food security.  
8 
 
● Livestock are roaming the streets and being hurt by vehicular moveme nts. 
Without a proper livestock policy the local or indigenous breed s buffalo, cow, 
ox, poultry, duck, pig, goat are loosing out on exotic breed thus eroding the 
biodiversity and making them more prone for pest and disease atta ck. There 
are fallow areas and abandoned field with abundance of  grass that could be 
harvested from fields and used as fodder but this is not done – often it is burnt. 
Solutions 
● Monitor and publicly share the current status of natural resource management 
(NRM) especially for areas like fisheries and water, such as groundwater 
availability and levels of extraction, marine and freshwater quality.  
● This data should be context-specific, regularly updated, and easy to access.  
● While this may not apply to all types of NRM (e.g., lists of  species in protected 
forests), where relevant, it is essential. 
Fisheries & Marine 
● Develop clear guidelines for sustainable fishing and shellfi sh farms/harvesting, 
utilising traditional knowledge and systems  whilst also making use of 
research and supporting new research on Goa’s ecological systems. 
● Take enforcement action proactively and impose heavy penalti es without 
awaiting complaints  by citizens on:  
o trawlers that engage in harmful illegal practices  
o strictly enforcing limits to the catch and harvesting of juvenile  fish and 
shellfish based on defined catch size of different species  
o over-fishing by foreign boats and enforce monsoon fishing ban 
o Strictly enforce the net mesh size regulations which are being flouted .

● Increase the number of High Speed Patrol/Interceptor Boats, so th at existing

4. Health 
Health outcomes are shaped far beyond the boundaries of the health sector—they 
depend on clean air and water, safe housing, nutritious food, green public spaces, 
and social inclusion. Goa is undergoing rapid urbanization, infrastructural expansion, 
and environmental degradation, which is significantly impacting public health. By 
aligning urban planning, climate action, and environmental protection with public 
health goals, Goa can significantly reduce disease burdens, prevent health crises, 
14 
 
and promote physical and mental well-being. Viksit Goa’s vision offers a powerful 
opportunity to embed health resilience into every facet of development, ensuring 
healthier lives for all its residents. 
Issues: 
● Environmental Health Risks: Urban expansion, traffic, waste, and construction 
have led to: 
● Air pollution is causing rising respiratory illnesses. 
● Contaminated water sources due to poor sewage increasing risk of 
diarrheal and vector-borne diseases 
● Mining and soil degradation from agrochemical use and burning of 
waste is increasing long-term exposure risks for cancers and 
reproductive health issues 
● Climate-Related Vulnerabilities: Goa faces rising temperatures, extreme 
weather events, and water stress: 
● Rising temperatures increase risks of dehydration, heat stress, 
heatstroke, and worsening chronic conditions. 
● Increased flooding creates risk of vector-borne and waterborne 
disease outbreaks. 
● Climate change may amplify antimicrobial resistance and disease 
outbreaks. 
- Lifestyle and Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs): Urban design and 
dependency on vehicles are contributing to: 
● Reduced physical activity due to lack of walkable, safe environments 
leading to obesity and increasing risk of Non Communicable Diseases 
(e.g. diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease). 
● Mental and Social Well-being: Loss of green space and public areas, 
noise, housing insecurity, loss of cultural identity and community 
fragmentation contribute .Stress, anxiety, social isolation, depression, 
particularly among youth and the elderly 
- Sustainable and Safe Food Sources

● Institute, monitor and enforce effective sewage management along the

● The size of panchayats and low salary levels combined with sign ificant 
powers leaves panchayats open to capture by powerful interest groups like 
property developers. 
● Meetings are often not advertised which means that many residen ts are not 
aware of meeting dates. Planning decisions are not made in public. 
● Panchayati Raj is supposed to be a participatory system with speci alist 
committees but most people are not skilled in managing participatory 
meetings and discussions get co-opted by the loudest voices preventing them 
from being the open Sabhas intended. Committees are often not operational. 
● Communidades in Goa as well as newer self-help groups have pl ayed an 
important role in rural development and maintaining infrastructure. 
Solutions 
29 
 
● develop forums for panchayats to work together across villages and with 
NGOs to develop their planning policies using evidence and consultation to do 
so.  
● ensure transparency in panchayat proceedings with planning decisions 
being made in public with papers posted on websites in advance of any 
decision-making meeting. All meetings to be on website and provision made 
for interested citizens to receive email alerts and notifications. 
● appeal rights to be provided to residents to challenge decisions and 
Vigilance Committee strengthened. 
● Agriculture Department to work with panchayat and communidade on 
repair and maintenance of khazans and to ensure they are protected from 
encroachment and infrastructure development, to create village action plans

● Panchayat committees to be strengthened by providing secretariat and

● Promote participation in panchayat affairs by local stakeholders by

facilities and infrastructure for disabled and aged also need to be strengthen 
at gram panchayat level. Ensure all citizen committees are active and 
contribute to participatory GPDP planning. 
 
15. Infrastructure development 
Issues: 
● Infrastructure projects such as roads, bridges and jetties are being developed 
without proper regard to the carrying capacity of Goa’s coastal strip, climate 
resilience needs, nor the loss of ecological and tourism benefits from the 
Western Ghats and inland Goa. Those inland areas will become more important 
to Goa as coastal erosion and sea level rise continue.  
● Impacts on the Western Ghats from road, rail and power infrastructure could be 
enormous. Wildlife need to be able to move and all of this infrastructure adds to 
fire risk which is a growing problem in Goa. 
● Goa’s coastline is extremely important given the state's reliance on the fisheries 
sector, coastal tourism and second homes/retirees. The protection of coastal 
habitats is of particular concern given projections of global heating and the 
consequent sea level rise that risks threatening hundreds of millions of people 
living in coastal and low-lying areas. In fact, large parts of Goa are predicted to 
be threatened with devastating coastal flooding by the end of the century. 
30 
 
These issues are exacerbated by inappropriate coastal infrastructure and the 
destruction of coastal ecosystems. 
● Climate resilience infrastructure is needed and this includes digital infrastructure 
like early warning systems. 
● Infrastructure such as roads and drains are carbon intensive using concrete and 
steel. Paving, road surfaces, drains are often impermeable reducing water 
percolation and groundwater recharge. 
● Traditional water capture and management systems are often in a poor state of 
repair and fields and areas that acted as recharge zones have been built upon. 
Goa suffers frequent water shortages and groundwater is being illegal extracted 
and sold to new developments, hotels and industry. 
Solutions 
Nature-based coastal erosion/flood protection infrastructure 
● Coastal erosion & flood protection infrastructure is required and this can 
be done using nature-based solutions. Sand dunes and mangroves have the 
potential to protect shores from rising sea levels making them vital for climate 
resilience. Create a more robust plan for the mapping and safeguarding of sand 
dunes and mangroves in Goa and enforcement plus restorative action taken 
where destroyed. 
● Ensure road surfaces, pavements, and drains are made of permeable 
materials which are now available in the market to maximise groundwater 
recharge and reduce flood risk. 
Align with safeguarding our natural assets 
● All infrastructure to be designed to minimise harm and regenerate natural 
ecosystems. For example roads should be built to be shaded and tree-lined 
and allow for animal movements. Roads can be used to channel water to ponds 
or aquifers. 
● Revisit and modify the construction projects in Mollem which will devastate parts 
of the Western Ghats 
Climate resilient systems - water infrastructure 
● Ensure every new housing or commercial development is aligned with 
better water management to include permeable surfaces, as well as swales 
and ponds or recharge zones. 
● Restore Goa’s system of ponds, bandaras and flooded fields that collected 
water and stored it in shallow aquifers. 
● Monitor water demand and scarcity that arises as well as pollution and take 
swift action to protect local “water assets”. 
● A transparent process and more detailed considerations for granting 
consent to borewells or renewal of licenses. Where a borewell has been 
31 
 
illegally constructed or allowed capacity exceeded, a license for that site should 
only be renewed under exceptional circumstances. The panchayat should make 
the list of borewell licenses already granted public (on its website) including the 
license holder and capacity limits and any new borewell application should be 
published in the Goa Gazette and on the community noticeboard and website 
with a 30 day period for comment. Building approval applications should also be 
made public in advance of approval stating the position on PWD water. 
Sustainable Low carbon materials 
● Measure the “embodied emissions” (related to making materials) of 
infrastructure projects and seek to minimise these emissions whether through 
better design or the use of lower carbon materials. For example it is possible to 
build walls using construction wastes and use stone or even earth rather than 
concrete in certain circumstances. 
Smart Risk Management Infrastructure 
● Identify and conduct a risk-assessment of human settlements, fisher 
communities and built heritage sites along Goa's coast / tidal rivers. Prepare 
specific comprehensive disaster management plans for the protection of these 
settlements and structures. Develop an efficient storm warning system and 
carry out a comprehensive all Goa mapping of sea level rise and it's impacts on 
Goa's coast (an example of this is Odisha’s Disaster Management System 
which has been lauded by the UN) 
● Develop a fire monitoring system and equipment to extinguish bush and 
forest fires quickly. 
 
16. Economy/Wealth Generation plus Entrepreneurship and Start-ups 
Issue 
● Goa needs a strategy for development aligned with its high  ambition in 
prosperity and the environment. At present there are insufficient opportunities 
for local people which leads to pressure to sell land to developers. It’s 
economy is increasingly dependent on land sales, speculative real estate, and 
seasonal tourism — creating an unsustainable, unequal growth model.  
● In the absence of regulation and incentives, market-related e conomic 
incentives and investment interests favour destructive development. 
● Growth in the right areas requires skills, investment in innovat ion, and science 
& technology research 
● Local participation in new wealth creation is limited — either due to skills gaps 
or lack of access to capital and networks. 
● High-potential, low-footprint economic models are under-leveraged — such as 
content creation, digital services, wellness retreats, and climate-resilient 
farming. 
 
Solutions 
32 
 
Strategic 
● Reimagine Goa’s economic development through a Clean Economy Growth 
Plan — where value creation is decoupled from land or extractive industry, 
working with citizens and business. 
● Build links with Universities to support research of relevance to Goa’s 
future, working with business incubators as well as different departments and 
faculties. 
● Create Land Use options to reduce pressure to sell: Develop land trusts, 
cooperatives and other viable options to reduce pressure to sell and enable 
people to choose a different development path. 
Capture opportunity - entrepreneurship and start-ups 
● Make Goa the testbed for low-footprint entrepreneurship — co-living hotels, 
sustainable brands, creator businesses, small-scale exporters, rural Software 
as a Service teams. 
● Identify areas of innovation where Goa can take the lead and set up centres 
of expertise that bring relevant bodies together and support 
incubators/accelerators in these fields. 
● Support young Goans to develop enterprise skills and create employment 
pipelines in green businesses - ensuring adequate skilling for green 
livelihoods eg solar panel maintenance, remote sensing, environmental 
assessments et 
● Combat AI redundancies by developing livelihoods for example in the 
care industries, increasingly important with levels of older people. 
Smart incentives 
● Create a Heritage-to-Income program — linking cultural homes, artisan work, 
and traditional industries with modern branding and ecommerce. This may 
include “open house week” and single tickets for multiple destinations. 
● Implement a Goa Performance Recognition and Reward model where local 
panchayats and citizens who contribute to clean, inclusive, and sustainable 
growth (as per data) receive bonus funds, tax reliefs, and infrastructure 
upgrades. 
● Create a reward and recognition scheme for the hospitality sector which 
links consumer interest in a clean, green environment with interest in raising 
standards. 
● Utilize Revolving Funds (RFs) or Circular Funds for climate change, which can 
be loaned out for infrastructure or climate change projects at low interest rates, 
especially for mitigation activities like renewable energy. 
● Implement a Green Tax on tourism to assist in mitigation and adaptation 
activities.

17. Public Transport 
Issues 
● Many commute to work but the public transport system is inadequate , forcing 
people into private cars and bikes. Buses are polluting, overcrowded and 
slow, preventing many from accessing work and study with impact on 
economic activity 
● Goa has limited landspace and giving up more and more space t o roads not 
only divides villages and communities, creates noise, dust and pollution 
generally, but also reduces land for housing.  
● Huge investment in roads currently but not designed using mode rn materials 
eg road surfaces that are porous for water, preventing flooding and recharging 
the water table. 
● Town and village roads are not pedestrian or cycle friendly wi th little shading, 
space, and often complex one -way systems. 
● Current road widening is happening to feed real estate d evelopment, often 
without the correct permissions and destroying village peace and quiet and 
cultural identity. Globally proven -  road widening does not reduce traffic. 
● Train infrastructure exists but is not being optimised. 
 
Solution 
● Create an inexpensive, regular, reliable bus network with comfortable bus 
stops (safe, shaded) and real time information at bus stops and on phones. 
● Enhance the last and first mile framework with support for mini-buses, 
rickshaws, pilots, as well as non-motorised transport -  bicycles including 
power-assisted bicycles - and safe shaded pedestrian routes. Electric or other 
low pollution rickshaws should be given incentives - higher subsidies or lower 
taxes - and Uber/Ola should be permitted only for green vehicles (hybrid or 
EV below a certain weight). 
● Introduce a coastal and hill town charge (tourist tax) as seen in hill stations 
like Panchgani.  
● Incentivize the use of small and low carbon vehicles with lower charges 
for smaller, less polluting vehicles - road tax, local charge and parking fees. 
● Optimise the  north-south rail route to be a viable way for people to travel 
include commuting to work.  
● Continue the take back subsidy for decommissioning of highly polluting 
buses and upgrading of buses. 
● Ensure the electric bus system is powered by renewable energy. 
● Trial electric ferries in particular solar powered ferries for travel along rivers, 
exploring both passenger and car ferries.

19. Government/Governance 
Issues:  
 
● There is a sense of a growing disconnect between government a nd people 
where private interests are being upheld over community interests.  
● A forward-looking and coherent economic and spatial planning  approach 
appears to be missing, and the interests of children and future generations 
are not taken on board   
● Fragmented policy responsibility, a lack of use of robust up to  date data, and 
limited participation in decision-making hampers decision-making creating a 
significant risk to Goa’s future.   
● Lack of  systems for working across different departments creates 
inefficiencies, overlaps, gaps and duplications.  
● The law is often not implemented or enforced in part becau se of lack of clarity 
about whose responsibility it is to  monitor non-compliance and enforce e.g. 
on pollution, groundwater use, and processes such as environmental impact 
assessments.  
● There is a widespread loss of trust in governance systems and a be lief that 
the law is often only implemented where citizens bring a challenge to the 
courts, putting undue responsibility on ordinary citizens. 
 
Solutions: 
 
35 
 
● Devolve power to Panchayats and urban local bodies to plan, making it legally 
binding as per the 73rd and 74th amendments to the Constitution.  
● In parallel ensure training is provided for officials and panch members on 
community leadership and on key policy areas - planning and green 
buildings, water management, sanitation, waste management and sustainable 
agriculture - as well as the participatory & secretarial skills needed for efficient 
management of the Gram Panchayat Development Planning process. 
● Require and support decisions being made openly and transparently, 
and publicly shared and monitored through transparent data platforms. 
● This should also be with regard to accurate and robust data. Finance should 
be made available for villages to collect data including measuring impact, 
consult in an open way, as well as to ensure websites are updated and 
actively used to engage people. 
● Bonuses should be given to panchayats who excel in transparency and 
inclusiveness, and in data-driven decision making. 
● The public should be enabled to make complaints easily which are 
responded to and acted upon quickly with a 2-day window for a reply, 
building on the Flying Squad principle of urgent action. 
● An Ombudsman should be established to enable complaints of 
“maladministration” to be pursued. It should have comprehensive jurisdiction 
and its orders implemented. 
● Support the creation of climate resilience plans specific for each town and 
village that prioritises the protection of natural habitats that reduce heat 
stress, support groundwater recharge and also support flood and storm 
mitigation strategies, consciously planned and with open and inclusive local 
participation 
● Support co-operation between departments, jurisdictions and levels to 
align climate change imperatives with planning & monitoring within each 
department, including implementation of the State Climate Action Plan with 
oversight by citizen-expert committees.  
● Ensure public participation of coastal communities prior to proposing 
developmental projects along the coast.

new economic opportunities while also reviving the old - farming and artisan skills - 
taking a leadership role as a centre of excellence of low carbon - low footprint 
industry and activity to support the shift we need to see. It is of a Goa that remains 
true to its culture of inclusiveness, harmony and an appreciation of aesthetic beauty 
and the sacredness of nature. It is also of a state that has shown boldness of spirit, a 
willingness to innovate, a confidence in our traditional culture as well as in our ability
