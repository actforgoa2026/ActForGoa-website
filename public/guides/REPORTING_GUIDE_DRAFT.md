# Report a civic or environmental problem in Goa

**Draft for Act for Goa. Source review: 22 October 2026.**

Use this guide to document a problem, choose an authority, submit a complaint and keep track of the response. It also explains how to request official records through the Right to Information Act. It covers Goa's village and municipal areas; the correct office depends on where the problem is and what it concerns.

**Draft status:** steps marked **Verification needed** require the checks listed in the [verification register](/read/guide-verification) before publication. Contact details below were checked against published sources, not tested by calling or submitting a case. These drafts do not determine whether a particular activity is unlawful.

## Start here

If someone is in immediate danger, move to safety and seek emergency assistance. Use Goa's official emergency directory for police, fire and ambulance assistance. An administrative complaint or RTI application is not an emergency response. [Official helplines][R1]

For other problems:

1. Record exactly what you observed and where.
2. Choose the responsible office using the table below.
3. Submit a short factual complaint with relevant evidence.
4. Keep proof of submission and a reference number.
5. Follow up using that same reference. Request records through RTI if you need documentary evidence of permissions or action taken.

These five steps are practical recommendations. They do not create a legal deadline or replace a prescribed application or appeal procedure.

## Choose where to report

| Problem | Starting point | What to ask for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missed waste collection, a public drain or another local sanitation problem | The Village Panchayat or municipal council/Corporation responsible for the location. If responsibility is unclear, use the CM Helpline to seek routing. | Identify the responsible service, inspect the location and give a reference for corrective action. |
| Smoke, waste burning, sewage discharge, industrial pollution or noise | Goa State Pollution Control Board (GSPCB); also identify the local body or utility responsible for the underlying service. | Inspection, appropriate measurements or sampling, verification of relevant permissions, and action within the authority's powers. |
| Suspected unauthorised construction in a village | Village Panchayat, addressed to its Secretary/Sarpanch. Send planning questions to the relevant TCP office or PDA as well. | Check the licence, approved plans and conditions; inspect the reported work. |
| Suspected unauthorised construction in a town | The municipal council's Chief Officer or the Corporation's designated building authority; the planning authority for planning issues. | Check permission and inspect. Margao publishes a procedure involving inspection, notice, hearing and an order; other towns must confirm their own route. |
| Tree felling or damage to forest land | The Forest Department office responsible for the location; ask for the Range Forest Officer/Tree Officer or the appropriate forest authority. | Verify permissions and conditions and inspect the reported activity. |
| Hill cutting or filling low-lying land | TCP, asking for the office dealing with section 17A; notify the local body about associated construction. | Verify the applicable land classification and prior permission. |
| Coastal work, wetland filling or interference with a watercourse | The relevant environmental authority: GCZMA for coastal regulation; the Wetland Authority for notified-wetland questions; Water Resources for its water-management functions. Use the Environment Department or CM Helpline to establish the correct office if uncertain. | Identify the applicable protection, responsible authority and permissions, and arrange inspection where warranted. |
| Suspected animal cruelty | Local police and appropriate veterinary/Animal Husbandry assistance. Wildlife cases also require the Forest Department. | Record the complaint, arrange appropriate assessment and consider action under the applicable law. |

Basis: [Panchayat charter, printed pages 19–20][R2]; [Margao construction complaints][R3]; [GSPCB][R4]; [TCP section 17A procedure][R5]; [Environment Department charter][R6]; [Goa animal-cruelty SOP][R7]. The table is a starting-point guide, not an exclusive allocation of legal jurisdiction.

## Use these published complaint channels

**Pollution:** GSPCB's website says complaints and grievances are accepted at **mail.gspcb@gov.in**. It also publishes an [online complaint page][R8] and a source-listed noise helpline. Keep the sent email and attachments; ask for the complaint number. The online page requires JavaScript. These are source-listed channels; receipt, staffing and resolution have not been tested. [GSPCB announcement][R4]

**General government grievances:** the [CM Helpline portal][R9] publishes its current contact route and describes online/phone registration with a tracking ID. Give the location, department if known, facts, requested action and supporting documents. Save the ID and check its status. Ask which department received the case.

**Verification needed R01:** confirm the live submission flow, receipt process and current contact details. Different government pages list different general grievance numbers; use the CM Helpline portal to confirm its own published contact route. The portal's advertised resolution estimates are not used here as statutory deadlines.

For paper submission, take a copy and request an inward stamp, date and reference on your copy. If posting, retain the delivery evidence. Where an office prescribes its own form, complete that form and attach the factual statement from the template.

## Prepare the evidence

Create one folder for the case. Include:

- Location: village/town, ward, taluka, a landmark and a map pin. Add survey/subdivision numbers if known; mark uncertain identifiers as unconfirmed.
- Dates and times, including whether the problem is continuing or recurring.
- A short account separating what you saw from what somebody told you.
- Original photos or video with a simple index describing each file. Preserve originals; use copies for annotations.
- Relevant letters, permission numbers or public notices, if available.
- The action you seek: inspection, verification of permission, repair, collection, sampling, a written decision or another specific response.

Observe from somewhere you may lawfully be. Do not enter a worksite or private property to obtain evidence, confront workers, or handle suspected hazardous material. Describe uncertainty plainly: “I could not see a permission board from the public road” is more accurate than “there is no permission.”

For noise, note the source, location, time and duration. A phone recording can support an account; do not present it as an official calibrated noise measurement. For suspected pollution, record visible or sensory observations and request official assessment rather than declaring the substance's composition.

## Write and submit the complaint

Use the [complaint template](/guides/templates/01-complaint.txt). A useful subject is:

```text
Request for inspection of [observed issue] at [location], observed on [date]
```

State the facts, attach an indexed evidence list and request an acknowledgement, inspection where appropriate, and a written response. If a person or company is unidentified, say so. Do not guess a name or allege an offence you cannot substantiate.

Provide the contact information needed for a response. If disclosure could expose you to retaliation, ask how your details will be handled; do not assume an ordinary complaint will remain anonymous or confidential. Keep unnecessary identity documents out of public copies.

## Follow up and escalate

Record the office, submission date, reference, promised response date and what happened. Use the [case log](/guides/templates/05-case-log.md). If the office supplies no timeframe, ask for one and set your own reminder; label that as a personal follow-up date, not a statutory deadline.

Use the [follow-up template](/guides/templates/02-follow-up.txt) when the promised date passes, the problem continues, or a closure message does not address it. Refer to the original complaint and attach only relevant new evidence. Ask for the inspection/action record and the next responsible officer.

**Village construction complaints:** the Directorate's charter describes approaching the **Block Development Officer** when the Panchayat fails to act, followed by the **Deputy Director of Panchayats** where inaction continues. Sections 66(5)–(7) of the Panchayat Act address supervisory action and appeals. An administrative escalation and an appeal against an order are different procedures. [Charter, printed pages 19–20][R2]; [Panchayat Act, section 66][R10]

**Verification needed R04:** confirm the current office, statutory provision and any relevant order before giving a case-specific appeal deadline. The charter is historical guidance. Do not apply this village route to municipal cases, and do not wait for a grievance response if an appeal period is already running.

For an unresolved departmental service issue, check its grievance officer and whether the particular service is covered by Goa's notified time-bound service framework. That framework does not automatically give every environmental complaint the same deadline. Seek legal advice promptly for a notice, appeal or urgent court remedy; the [Goa legal-aid service][R11] explains eligibility and available assistance.

## Use RTI when you need official records

An RTI application asks a public authority for information it holds. It does not itself order a site inspection or stop construction. Indian citizens can use the Act. [RTI Act, sections 3 and 6][R12]

Ask for identifiable records and a defined period. Useful requests include:

- Copies of the planning permission, building licence, sanctioned plans and amendments for a specified property.
- Inspection reports, notices, recorded decisions and action-taken records concerning a numbered complaint.
- The applicable rule, circular, published fee schedule or officer-designation record.

Use the [RTI template](/guides/templates/03-rti-records-request.txt), addressed to the public authority's State Public Information Officer (SPIO). It asks for existing records, allows partial disclosure where appropriate and requests transfer of relevant parts if another public authority holds them. Exemptions and third-party procedures can affect disclosure; do not promise access to every record.

The [Goa RTI Online portal][R13] advertises application and appeal services. Its FAQ describes first appeals using the original registration number and email. **Verification needed R02:** direct retrieval of the portal failed during this review, so confirm the live authority list, upload limits, fee/payment route and paper alternative before publication. A portal outage does not remove the option of writing to the correct SPIO.

The Goa fee rules in the corpus specify a **₹10 application fee** and address further charges. Confirm the presently accepted payment method and any applicable copying/certification charge; keep the receipt. The Act provides a fee exemption for people below the poverty line, subject to the applicable proof requirements. [Goa fee rules, rule 3][R14]; [RTI Act, section 7(5)][R12]

## Keep RTI deadlines separate from complaint follow-up

| RTI stage | Statutory starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary SPIO response | Generally within 30 days of receipt. |
| Information concerning life or liberty | 48 hours; this concerns information, not emergency intervention. |
| Transfer to another public authority | As soon as practicable, within 5 days. |
| First appeal | Ordinarily within 30 days after the response deadline expires or the decision is received. |
| First-appeal decision | Normally 30 days; up to 45 with reasons recorded. |
| Second appeal | Ordinarily within 90 days of when the first-appeal decision should have been made or was received. |

Source: [RTI Act, sections 6(3), 7 and 19][R12]. Calculate the deadline for the actual case: APIO submission adds time, additional-fee notices can pause the response clock, and third-party procedures have separate provisions. Delayed appeals may be admitted for sufficient cause; do not depend on that discretion.

**Verification needed R05:** the time limits above were checked in the collected official Act text. Compare it with the latest consolidated Act before publication, especially the disclosure provisions; the newer India Code download was unavailable during this review.

If there is no timely response or the answer is incomplete, use the [first-appeal template](/guides/templates/04-rti-first-appeal.txt) and identify the exact unanswered items. Keep the original application, receipt, response and relevant dates. The portal FAQ says it charges no first-appeal fee; reconfirm the current route under R02. A second appeal requires its own current Commission procedure and is outside this template pack.

## A short worked example

You see soil being tipped into a low-lying area beside your village road. Record the location and dates from the public road. Describe the tipping and visible drainage obstruction; do not label the land a notified wetland without records. Ask TCP about section 17A and notify the local body about the drainage concern. Seek environmental-authority routing if protection may apply. Submit the complaint, preserve the receipt and use a separate RTI request for permissions and inspection records. Track the complaint and RTI on separate lines in the case log.
