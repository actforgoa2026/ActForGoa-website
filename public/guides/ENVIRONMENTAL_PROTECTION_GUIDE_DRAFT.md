# Protect trees, water, coast and biodiversity in Goa

**Draft reviewed against collected sources on 22 October 2026. Site classifications, current orders and submission channels still need verification.**

Begin with the place and the observed activity. A tree-felling permission, building licence, coastal clearance and pollution consent answer different questions. Several protections may apply to the same site. Use this guide to gather the relevant records, request inspection and participate in a decision; a map pin alone cannot establish a breach.

## 1. Record the place and choose the authority

Use the [site and protection checklist](/guides/templates/15-environment-site-check.md). Record village/town, taluka, survey/subdivision number if known, a map pin, landmarks, dates and what you observed. Mark approximate locations and uncertain boundaries explicitly. Photographs from public or otherwise lawful positions can show activity without establishing its legal classification.

| Observed issue | First authority or record-holder to identify |
| --- | --- |
| Tree cutting, suspected forest clearance, wildlife habitat damage | Jurisdictional Forest office/Tree Officer; ask about forest and protected-area status as well as felling permission. |
| Coastal, estuarine, mangrove or dune development | Goa Coastal Zone Management Authority (GCZMA); local planning/body records may also matter. |
| Lake or suspected wetland filling | Goa State Wetland Authority (GSWA), with TCP/local-body referral where land development is involved. |
| Hill cutting or low-lying land filling | TCP for section 17A; other authorities according to forest, coastal, wetland or water impacts. |
| Sewage, discharge, emissions, waste dumping or noise | GSPCB and the relevant local body or utility, according to the source and service responsibility. |
| Blocked channel, water abstraction or well works | Water Resources Department and the relevant local/revenue authority; ask which office controls the watercourse or activity. |
| A proposed project or draft notification | The authority and submission channel named in the actual consultation notice. |

Official starting points are the [Forest Department][EN1], [environment department charter][EN2], [GCZMA][EN3], [TCP procedure][EN4], [GSPCB][EN5] and [WRD legislation index][EN6].

**Verification needed EP01:** confirm the precise jurisdiction, current officer and accepted channel. A local body and a specialist regulator may both have roles; a copy to one is not proof that the other registered the case. Ask each office to identify the part it will address.

For immediate danger to people or a fire, use emergency services. For continuing environmental damage, request urgent inspection and explain what is changing; the urgency label does not itself create a stop-work order.

## 2. Check tree permissions and forest status separately

Goa's Preservation of Trees Act contains restrictions on felling/removal and a permission process, with exceptions and conditions. Begin by asking the Tree Officer whether the actual operation requires permission and whether an order exists. Ownership of the land does not by itself settle that question. [Trees Act, sections 8–10][EN7]

Request the application, site sketch, inspection report, tree inventory, permission/refusal, conditions, validity, extension and any transport permission applicable to the produce. Compare the approved location, species, number and identifiers with what can be observed safely. A photograph or rough tree count is a reason to ask for inspection, not proof that the entire operation is unlawful.

For somebody seeking permission, Goa Online provides **Form B** and an official checklist describes application, verification, inspection and approval stages. Use the prescribed route with the current required ownership/authority documents and site details; confirm payment only through the official process. [Goa Online service][EN8]; [official checklist][EN9]

**Verification needed EP02:** check the current Act/rules, subsequent notifications, species exceptions, emergency-tree procedure, co-owner requirements, felling and transit conditions, fees and appeal rights. The checklist contains overlapping tree-count bands, so this draft does not reproduce its approval thresholds. Do not rely on elapsed time alone as permission or attempt emergency felling using this guide.

If forest status is uncertain, ask Forest authorities for the applicable records and orders, including any private-forest identification, protected-area boundary or eco-sensitive-zone notification. A revenue entry or visible absence of dense trees does not resolve all forest questions. The Forest website publishes separate private-forest and protected-area resources. [Forest Department][EN1]

Act for Goa already has [tree-cutting guidance](https://actforgoa.org/resource/how-to-stop-tree-cutting/) and a [reporting resource](https://actforgoa.org/resource/report-tree-cutting/). Use the evidence approach, while checking every legal citation and contact against current official material.

## 3. Establish the coastal plan and clearance applying to the site

Open the [GCZMA website][EN3] and its [Final CZMP Goa document page][EN10]. Ask for the **approved plan sheet, approval letter, amendments/corrections and applicable CRZ notification** for the exact location. Record the map edition, scale and authority. A page heading, draft sheet or third-party overlay is not proof of the operative boundary.

Request the project's coastal application, authorised demarcation/site plan where applicable, clearance or refusal, conditions and inspection records. Compare the actual activity with the permission's stated purpose and location. Also check the separate planning, local-body and other environmental permissions relevant to the work.

Do not infer one universal distance from the sea or river that makes construction lawful. Category, tidal influence, feature, approved map, dates and the activity all matter. The corpus contains a central CRZ 2019 compilation, but its existence does not establish which approved Goa plan applies to a particular parcel. [Central CRZ compilation][EN11]

**Verification needed EP03:** obtain the current Goa approval package, applicable notification and amendments, corrected site sheet, relevant court orders and qualified interpretation of any contested demarcation. No parcel classification or coastal setback is certified here.

## 4. Distinguish a wetland record from a final notification

Ask GSWA for the site's name and identifier, inventory/brief document, any Ramsar designation or final notification, schedules, boundary and zone-of-influence map, and later amendments or orders. Ask which restrictions and management authority apply.

The Wetlands Rules 2017 distinguish their definition and scope from ordinary descriptions of wet ground. Rule 2 excludes categories including paddy fields and river channels; rule 3 addresses applicability and areas covered by specified other regimes. For wetlands within the rules' scope, rule 4 includes prohibitions such as conversion/encroachment, solid-waste dumping and untreated waste discharge. Site facts and overlapping laws still matter. [Wetlands Rules, rules 2–4][EN12]

**A draft notification is a proposal, not the final notification.** The corpus's March 2023 Goa gazette expressly invites objections and suggestions. Use it to trace the process and obtain the eventual outcome; do not cite it as proof that its proposed schedules became operative. Conversely, absence from one final-notification list does not prove that a place has no protection under another law or court direction. [Goa draft notifications][EN13]; [implementation guidance][EN14]

The GSWA [gazette index][EN15] is a discovery route. Direct retrieval failed during review. If it remains unavailable, ask GSWA or the environment department for the numbered final notification and maps, using the [records and inspection request](/guides/templates/16-environment-records-and-inspection.txt) and, if necessary, the RTI route in the [reporting guide](/read/guide-reporting).

**Verification needed EP04:** verify final versus draft status, amendments, applicable court protection, boundary, zone of influence and the current GSWA contact/submission channel. The national guidance does not determine an individual lake's present status.

## 5. Report pollution, filling or obstruction with specific observations

For suspected pollution, record the location and apparent source, date/time, duration, visible discharge, odour, affected water or land, and any recurring pattern. Request official inspection or sampling. Avoid claiming a chemical identity or statutory measurement from a photograph, smell or phone application.

GSPCB publishes **mail.gspcb@gov.in** for complaints/grievances. Save the sent message and attachments and ask for a reference. Its online complaint page requires a usable browser session. Use the local body for a collection/drainage service issue and GSPCB for the pollution concern as appropriate. [GSPCB][EN5]

For hill cutting or low-lying filling, identify both the excavation/fill location and any affected drain, stream or neighbouring land. TCP publishes a separate section 17A permission process. Ask for that permission and its approved plan/conditions without assuming it replaces other required approvals. [TCP section 17A][EN4]

For a blocked watercourse or abstraction, ask WRD to identify the competent office, relevant record and inspection route. Do not excavate a new channel, collect hazardous samples or enter flowing/flooded water to prove a case. [WRD legal framework][EN6]

**Verification needed EP05:** confirm live complaint handling, the applicable consent or authorisation, current pollution/waste requirements and the inspection/sampling authority. No universal response period or automatic enforcement outcome is claimed.

## 6. Make a useful consultation submission

GSPCB publishes project-specific public-hearing notices. A hearing is part of the decision process where required; it is not itself the final environmental clearance. Start from the actual notice and project identity. [Official public-hearing example][EN16]

1. Save the notice and record the project/proponent, survey numbers, document locations, hearing date/venue and submission deadline. Check for postponements and corrigenda.
2. Obtain the available EIA, summary, maps and related application documents. Note the language and version used, and request access through the named authority if material is missing.
3. Use the [consultation submission](/guides/templates/17-environment-consultation.txt). Identify a specific impact, the page/map it concerns, local evidence and the answer or change sought.
4. Submit through the method in the notice, retain receipt, and request that written points be considered and recorded. If speaking at a hearing, bring a written copy.
5. Obtain the hearing proceedings, responses and eventual decision. Track conditions and compliance separately from the consultation.

Useful questions concern drainage/flooding, water demand, waste destination, noise, traffic, habitat, alternatives and monitoring. State which observations you can substantiate. A general objection becomes more useful when it identifies the record, impact and requested decision.

**Verification needed EP06:** confirm whether consultation is required for the actual project, the current EIA framework and exemptions, notice language, deadlines and how corrections are handled. Do not reuse an expired hearing notice or a draft-law consultation window.

## 7. Use community biodiversity knowledge carefully

Ask the local Biodiversity Management Committee (BMC) for its current contact, meeting process and People's Biodiversity Register (PBR) route. GSBB describes BMC conservation/documentation functions and PBRs as records of local resources, uses and traditional knowledge. [BMC guidance][EN17]; [PBR guidance][EN18]

Offer dated observations, photographs, habitat descriptions and a clear request to record or consider the issue. Ask how corrections and sensitive knowledge are handled. Obtain permission before sharing somebody else's traditional knowledge, and avoid publicly revealing vulnerable wildlife locations. A PBR entry can support documentation; it is not a substitute for a notification, permit or specialist enforcement decision.

**Verification needed EP07:** confirm the current committee, meeting and records-access process, and any access/benefit-sharing or research permission applying to collection or commercial use. This guide does not authorise collecting biological material.

## 8. Follow the decision and protect appeal time

Keep one case log with every authority, receipt, inspection, notice and decision. Use the [reporting guide](/read/guide-reporting) for follow-up and RTI records requests. Ask for a reasoned written outcome and check whether stated remedial work actually happened.

If irreversible damage is continuing, or you receive a permission/order you may need to challenge, seek prompt advice on the correct statutory appeal, tribunal or court route. The NGT Act distinguishes environmental disputes, relief and appeals; not every complaint belongs in the same proceeding. An ordinary grievance or pending RTI request should not be assumed to suspend a legal filing period. [NGT Act, sections 14–16][EN19]

**Verification needed EP08:** a practitioner must verify jurisdiction, standing, cause-of-action/communication dates, limitation, permitted extensions and the current filing procedure. The fresh India Code PDF download timed out. No universal appeal deadline or guarantee of a stop order is supplied.

## Worked example and templates

If trucks are filling a field beside a lake, record the field and lake separately, their locations and the activity. Ask TCP about section 17A; ask GSWA for the lake's final status and boundary; refer possible blocked drainage to the responsible water/local authority. Ask GSPCB to inspect if waste is being dumped. Attach the same factual evidence index to each relevant request, identify the issue each office should decide, and track their separate responses.
